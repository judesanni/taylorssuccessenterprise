Cookies & technology

Cookie Policy

This page describes the technologies used by the current version of this website and what may change if additional tools are added.

Last updated: October 23, 2026

What are cookies?

Cookies are small files that a website or service may store in your browser. Similar technologies can include local storage, pixels, and scripts that help a service remember settings, measure usage, or deliver advertising.

What this website currently uses

The current public website does not intentionally install its own analytics or advertising cookies and does not include an advertising pixel. The pages use ordinary HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for navigation, animation, and image display. Netlify may process technical information needed to deliver and protect the site and to handle form submissions.

The website requests some images from Unsplash and fonts from Google Fonts. Those third-party servers may receive technical request information, including your IP address and browser details, when the resources load. If you follow a link to Eventbrite, Instagram, or another third-party site, that service may use its own cookies or similar technologies under its own policy.

Managing cookies

You can usually block or delete cookies through your browser settings. Blocking some technologies may affect features on third-party websites you visit. Since the current site does not intentionally use non-essential analytics or advertising cookies, it does not display a separate cookie-consent banner in this version. If analytics, advertising, embedded social media, or other non-essential tracking is added later, this policy and any required consent controls should be updated before launch of those tools.

Changes and contact

We may update this policy if the website's technology changes. For questions, contact tsenterprisesatlanta@gmail.com. Please also review the Privacy Policy for details about inquiry forms and personal information.

This page is a practical starting point for the current website setup, not legal advice. The business should review it with a qualified attorney before relying on it as a complete compliance document, especially if its services, data collection, tracking technologies, or operating locations change.
